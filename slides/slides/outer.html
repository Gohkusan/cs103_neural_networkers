[Zhe An, about 45 s] Once we have delta, the weight gradient is one line. The derivative with respect to weight w_ij is delta_i times the input a_j, the error coming out of neuron i times the signal going into it. Collected into a matrix, that is the outer product delta times x-transpose: a 2 by 1 times a 1 by 2 gives a 2 by 2 matrix whose columns are all multiples of delta, so it has rank 1. For a batch we get Delta times A-transpose, and by the Week 2 column-row expansion that is the sum of m rank-1 outer products, one per sample; in our XOR batch only [1, 1] has an error, so the batch gradient equals this one. Taking one step with eta = 0.1, the total XOR loss drops from 0.5 to 0.48185. Be honest about the detail: [1, 1] improves a lot, but [0, 1] and [1, 0], which were already right, get worse, because all samples share the same weights. One step lowers the total, not every sample's loss. This loss is the sum over the four samples, not the average.
