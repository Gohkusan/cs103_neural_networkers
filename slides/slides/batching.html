[Gong Zu, about 40 s] Slide 4 already did this for four samples; here is why it matters at scale. For a 784 to 128 to 10 network and 1024 inputs, the work is about 208 million floating-point operations whether we loop over inputs or not. Batching does not reduce arithmetic. What changes is memory traffic: a matrix-vector product loads every weight to use it once, while a matrix-matrix product, called GEMM, loads each weight once and reuses it across all 1024 columns. On our 2-core CPU sandbox the single product was about 6 times faster on best times and about 7.5 times on medians, even single-threaded, so the gain is not parallelism. We did not measure a GPU; the same reuse argument is why GPUs make deep learning practical, and NVIDIA's guide notes matrix-vector work is always memory-limited.
