New · loss and gradient descent

Learning is walking downhill on the loss

ℓ = ½ Σⱼ (ŷⱼ − yⱼ)²

The loss ℓ: one number for how wrong the network is (here summed over the batch, no 1/m).

W ← W − η ∂ℓ/∂W

η = learning rate (step size). ∂ℓ/∂W has the same shape as W: entry (i, j) is ∂ℓ/∂wᵢⱼ.

Why minus the gradient?

For a small step, the change in ℓ ≈ inner product of gradient and step. Cauchy–Schwarz: most negative when the step points opposite the gradient.

Why is the gradient affordable?

Backprop: the whole gradient for under 6× (typically 2–3×) one forward pass. Nudging weights one by one: 101,770 extra passes for 784 → 128 → 10.

[Gong Zu, about 40 s] So far the weights were given to us. Learning means choosing them. First we need one number that says how wrong we are: the loss, here half the sum of squared errors over the batch. Then gradient descent: subtract eta times the gradient. The gradient with respect to a matrix is a matrix of the same shape, one partial derivative per weight. Why the minus sign: for a small step, the change in loss is roughly the inner product of the gradient with the step, and by Cauchy-Schwarz that is most negative when we step opposite the gradient. Why it is affordable: backpropagation gets every partial derivative at once for a small constant multiple of one forward pass, under 6 and typically 2 to 3, per Baydin et al. 2018. Nudging each weight separately would need one extra forward pass per parameter, over a hundred thousand for this small network.
