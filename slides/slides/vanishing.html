[Haide, about 40 s] Look again at the backprop rule: every layer multiplies the error by a W-transpose and by phi-prime. Writing phi-prime as a diagonal matrix D makes it a pure product of matrices. Now a deliberate simplification, so we label it as intuition: suppose every layer had the same W and phi-prime were 1. Then the error is multiplied by W-transpose to the power k, and from Week 11, if W = S Lambda S-inverse, that power is S-inverse-transpose Lambda-to-the-k S-transpose. Eigenvalues with absolute value below 1 shrink to zero: the vanishing gradient, where early layers barely learn. Above 1 they blow up: the exploding gradient. The activation makes it worse: the logistic derivative is at most one quarter, so ten logistic layers can shrink the error by about a million from those factors alone. That is one reason ReLU, whose derivative is 1 where active, became standard. Real networks have different matrices per layer; Pascanu, Mikolov and Bengio 2013 give the rigorous version using norms.
